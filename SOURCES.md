## 1 — APEC day two in Shenzhen: a first-ever Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral is due (lead)
On the second day of APEC in Shenzhen, leaders gathered for the plenary
session; Xi Jinping addressed the plenary on the morning of 19 November.
Donald Trump's bilateral with Xi was scheduled for 12:45 Shenzhen time, and
the first-ever three-way meeting of Trump, Xi and Vladimir Putin was due at
about 14:30 Shenzhen time (06:30 UTC) at Shenzhen's Kylin Villa, according
to the White House schedule — with nuclear weapons, the Iran war, trade and
AI on the agenda. At the time of writing the trilateral had not yet taken
place; the image says it is due this afternoon.
Sources: The Guardian live blog (first published 19 Nov, 01:24 GMT, updated
01:52 GMT); The Hindu BusinessLine (16 Nov); Reuters via World of Now
(18 Nov); Asia Today (16 Nov).

## 2 — Grand Theft Auto VI launches worldwide
Grand Theft Auto VI launched at midnight local time in every region on
19 November, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, with the rollout beginning
in New Zealand; the standard edition costs US$79.99 and there is no PC
release date yet. By midday in Paris, nearly 2.3 million people were watching
channels broadcasting the game. The launch follows a day after review
embargoes lifted, with the game scoring 99 on Metacritic.
Sources: northjersey.com (18 Nov, via Reuters and Rockstar Games); People
(19 Nov); Le Monde (19 Nov); El Paso Times (18 Nov).

## 3 — Anthropic's shares debut on the Nasdaq as ANT
Anthropic's shares were due to start trading on the Nasdaq on 19 November
under the ticker ANT, a day after the company priced its IPO at US$83 a
share — above the US$70–78 range — in an enlarged offering of 980 million
shares worth about US$81.3 billion, the largest IPO ever, valuing the company
at about US$1.5 trillion. A concurrent US$26 billion private placement from
strategic investors including Google, Nvidia and Microsoft was also reported,
and the co-founders were to ring the opening bell.
Sources: BusinessWorld / Reuters (19 Nov); FinanceFeeds (19 Nov); PYMNTS
(19 Nov); moneyta.co (19 Nov).

## 4 — Bangladesh: Awami League lockdown day
The Awami League's nationwide lockdown fell on 19 November, with crude-bomb
blasts reported in Dhaka and 565 people arrested in the capital in 24 hours,
including 71 members of the Awami League and its student wing — among them
former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid, detained from his Dhaka home at
midnight. Overnight, the ancestral home of interim leader Muhammad Yunus in
Chattogram was torched; the fire was reported at about 23:30 local time on
18 November and first covered on 19 November. No death toll is claimed here:
a "4 killed" figure circulating in one low-quality aggregator could not be
verified.
Sources: The Business Standard (tbsnews.net, 19 Nov); The Daily Jagran
(19 Nov); worldeyes.in (19 Nov); tml.news (18 Nov).

## 5 — South Korea sits its CSAT university entrance exam
South Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test — the Suneung — was held on
19 November, with 551,864 candidates at 1,312 test centres, running 08:40 to
17:45. Security against AI cheating was tightened: glasses and button
inspections, 8,400 extra supervisors and 1,800 staff for smart-device checks.
Twenty-two candidates were caught with smartphones after the ministry sent
a disaster alert at 08:30 designed to ring unsubmitted phones. Flights were
halted 13:05–13:40 for the English listening section, and the stock market
opened an hour late, at 10:00.
Sources: AJU Press (19 Nov, 09:04 and 09:24); Korea JoongAng Daily
(19 Nov); Yonhap via korean-vibe.com (19 Nov); bizzbuzz.news / Yonhap
(19 Nov).

## 6 — Cyclone Akvan eases; Friday landfall now seen at 90–110 km/h
The India Meteorological Department's 5:30 am bulletin on 19 November said
Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan was easing over the Bay of Bengal, about 470 km
east-southeast of Narsapur at 02:30, and was forecast to make landfall
between Narsapur (Andhra Pradesh) and Puri (Odisha) by noon on Friday
20 November as a severe cyclonic storm, with winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to
110 — down from the earlier very-severe forecast of 120–130 km/h. Odisha shut
schools in six districts for three days and evacuated 50,000 people; red and
orange rain alerts were in force.
Sources: India Meteorological Department bulletins via fisheriesindia.com and
transclew.com (19 Nov); The Daily Jagran (19 Nov, 07:25 IST); nationpress.com
(18 Nov).

## 7 — India and Japan hold their fourth 2+2 dialogue in Tokyo
India's Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S.
Jaishankar co-chaired the fourth India–Japan 2+2 ministerial dialogue in
Tokyo on 19 November, with Japan's Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and
Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi, reviewing defence and strategic
cooperation and a free and open Indo-Pacific. It is Singh's third bilateral
with Koizumi this year. Singh toured the JMSDF base at Yokosuka with Koizumi
the day before.
Sources: Press Information Bureau, Government of India; The Hindu
BusinessLine (18 Nov); alojapan.com (18 Nov); PTI via kalimpongonlinenews
(19 Nov).

## 8 — New Zealand 140/5 at tea in the Wellington Test (brief)
India won the toss and bowled first on day one of the first Test at the Basin
Reserve, Wellington. Bumrah (3/31) and debutant Anshul Kamboj reduced New
Zealand to 60/4 at lunch; Henry Nicholls (66 not out) and Glenn Phillips
(42 not out) fought back to 140/5 at tea.
Sources: IANS via hiindia.com (19 Nov, lunch and tea reports); ANI via
freeaitoolsayoob.com (19 Nov); ESPNcricinfo live blog (19 Nov).

## 9 — Asian markets rise on the Anthropic IPO (brief)
Asian equities and US stock futures rose on Thursday after Anthropic priced
the biggest IPO ever; Japan's Nikkei rose 1% in early trading, Nasdaq
futures gained 1% and S&P futures 0.6%. The mood stayed fragile elsewhere:
South Korea's Kospi was flat and Taiwan stocks 1% lower.
Sources: Reuters via northlandnewsradio.com (19 Nov); Reuters via SRN News
(19 Nov).

## 10 — Para table tennis finals close the Pattaya championships (brief)
The ITTF World Para Table Tennis Championships ran in Pattaya, Thailand,
from 13 to 19 November, with 334 athletes from 64 nations contesting 37 medal
events — the first edition with full gender parity. The final day of play,
including the singles finals, fell on 19 November.
Sources: Table Tennis Australia; Table Tennis Ireland; Wikipedia (World Para
Table Tennis Championships); Table Tennis New Zealand.

## 11 — US AI-chip rule takes effect (brief)
The US Bureau of Industry and Security's interim final rule "Addressing
Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", issued 16 November, takes effect
on Federal Register publication, scheduled for 19 November. It requires
licences for advanced-chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore,
Thailand and Brazil, and for data centres giving D:5-headquartered companies
remote access to more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP of compute; it also
formally rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule.
Sources: Baker McKenzie sanctions blog (18 Nov); kurums.com (18 Nov);
aiworldwire.org (18 Nov); vectorwire.ai (18 Nov).

## 12 — EU Parliament committee votes today on the Space Act (brief)
The European Parliament's Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE)
was due to vote on Thursday 19 November, 09:20–10:30 in Brussels, on the
draft report on the safety, resilience and sustainability of space activities
— the EU Space Act — by rapporteur Elena Donazzan, after the political groups
reached a deal on the text on 4 November.
Sources: European Parliament news (17 Nov); US Office of Space Commerce
(17 Nov); Agence Europe (18 Nov); Advanced Television (6 Nov).

## 13 — Colombia strike: unions march against the Economic Rescue Law (brief)
Unions led by the CUT and the teachers' federation Fecode called a national
strike for Thursday 19 November against the government's Economic Rescue Law
(bill 477), a fiscal package that would raise VAT on some everyday goods from
5% to 19% and lift health contributions for some workers. In Bogotá the main
march leaves Parque Nacional at 09:00 for the Plaza de Bolívar; former
president Gustavo Petro said he would join the marches.
Sources: colombiaunfiltered.live (18 Nov); deeparrival.com; icp.org.tr;
democrata.es (5 Nov).
