## 1 — APEC day two in Shenzhen: a first-ever Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral is due (lead)
On the second day of APEC in Shenzhen, leaders gathered for the family photo
and the plenary session; Xi Jinping addressed the plenary on the morning of
19 November. Donald Trump's bilateral with Xi was scheduled for 12:45
Shenzhen time, and the first-ever three-way meeting of Trump, Xi and Vladimir
Putin was due at about 14:30 Shenzhen time (06:30 UTC) at Shenzhen's Kylin
Villa, according to the White House schedule — with Ukraine, the Iran war,
trade, AI and nuclear weapons on the agenda. At the time of writing the
trilateral had not yet taken place; the image says it is due this afternoon.
Sources: The Guardian live blog (first published 19 Nov, 01:24 GMT, updated
01:52 GMT); AJU Press (19 Nov); France 24 (19 Nov); BBC (19 Nov).

## 2 — Grand Theft Auto VI launches worldwide
Grand Theft Auto VI launched at midnight local time in every region on
19 November, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, with the rollout beginning
in New Zealand; the standard edition costs US$79.99 and there is no PC
release date yet. The launch follows a day after review embargoes lifted,
with the game scoring 99 on Metacritic.
Sources: Reuters via northjersey.com (19 Nov); Desert Sun (19 Nov);
Rockstar Games via Jagran Josh (19 Nov).

## 3 — Anthropic's shares debut on the Nasdaq as ANT
Anthropic's shares were due to start trading on the Nasdaq on 19 November
under the ticker ANT, a day after the company priced its IPO at US$83 a
share — above the US$70–78 range — in an enlarged offering of 980 million
shares worth about US$81.3 billion, the largest IPO ever, valuing the company
at about US$1.5 trillion. A concurrent US$26 billion private placement from
strategic investors including Google, Nvidia and Microsoft was also reported,
and the co-founders were to ring the opening bell.
Sources: BusinessWorld / Reuters (19 Nov); FinanceFeeds (19 Nov); The Desk
(19 Nov); moneyta.co (19 Nov).

## 4 — Bangladesh: Awami League lockdown day
The Awami League's nationwide lockdown fell on 19 November, with crude-bomb
blasts reported in Dhaka and 565 people arrested in the capital in 24 hours,
including 71 members of the Awami League and its student wing. Overnight, the
ancestral home of interim leader Muhammad Yunus in Chattogram was torched;
the fire was reported at about 23:30 local time on 18 November and first
covered on 19 November. No death toll is claimed here: a "4 killed" figure
circulating in one low-quality aggregator could not be verified.
Sources: The Business Standard (tbsnews.net, 19 Nov); worldeyes.in (19 Nov);
timesofprime9.com (19 Nov).

## 5 — South Korea sits its CSAT university entrance exam
South Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test — the Suneung — was held on
19 November, with 551,864 candidates at 1,312 test centres, running 08:40 to
17:45. Security against AI cheating was tightened after a tax-accountant
cheating confession on 15 November: glasses and button inspections, 8,400
extra supervisors and 1,800 staff for smart-device checks. Flights were
halted 13:05–13:40 for the English listening section, and the stock market
opened an hour late, at 10:00.
Sources: AJU Press (19 Nov, 09:04 and 09:24); tutubi.ph / Yonhap (19 Nov);
beforeyougokorea.com (19 Nov).

## 6 — Cyclone Akvan eases; Friday landfall now seen at 90–110 km/h
The India Meteorological Department's 5:30 am bulletin on 19 November said
Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan was easing over the Bay of Bengal, about 470 km
east-southeast of Narsapur at 02:30, and was forecast to make landfall
between Narsapur (Andhra Pradesh) and Puri (Odisha) by noon on Friday
20 November as a severe cyclonic storm, with winds of 90–100 km/h gusting to
110 — down from the earlier very-severe forecast of 120–130 km/h. Odisha shut
schools in six districts for three days and evacuated 50,000 people; red and
orange rain alerts were in force.
Sources: India Meteorological Department bulletins via fisheriesindia.com and
transclew.com (19 Nov); The Daily Jagran (19 Nov, 07:25 IST).

## 7 — India and Japan hold their fourth 2+2 dialogue in Tokyo
India's Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S.
Jaishankar co-chaired the fourth India–Japan 2+2 ministerial dialogue in
Tokyo on 19 November with Japan's Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and
Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi, reviewing defence and strategic
cooperation and a free and open Indo-Pacific. Singh's bilateral with Koizumi
was their third this year; Jaishankar also held the 19th Foreign Ministers'
Strategic Dialogue.
Sources: Press Information Bureau (19 Nov); Hindu BusinessLine (19 Nov);
Press Trust of India (19 Nov).

## 8 — New Zealand v India, first Test, day one in Wellington
India won the toss and bowled first on day one of the first Test at
Wellington. New Zealand reached 60 for 4 at lunch (23 overs): Jasprit Bumrah
took 2 for 17, dismissing Tom Latham for a duck in the first over; Akash Deep
took one wicket and debutant Anshul Kamboj one; Henry Nicholls was 29 not
out. (One outlet wrongly said Kamboj "struck twice"; its own scorecard shows
Kamboj 1–11, and the ANI figures are used here.)
Sources: ANI (19 Nov); IANS (19 Nov); CricSearch (19 Nov).

Asian stocks rose on 19 November on the Anthropic listing: the Nikkei was up

## 10 — Para table tennis finals close the Pattaya championships
The ITTF World Para Table Tennis Championships in Pattaya (13–19 November)
concluded on 19 November with the singles finals, the first of them starting
at 11:45 local time. The semi-finals were played on 18 November — for
example Giada Rossi beat Cátia Oliveira 3–1 in the women's class 2
semi-final. No winners are claimed here: the finals had not been played when

Sources: ITTF YouTube (19 Nov); Eleven Table Tennis (19 Nov); Table Tennis
Australia (19 Nov).

## 11 — US AI-chip remote-access rule takes effect
The US Bureau of Industry and Security's interim final rule on remote access
to advanced chips took effect on 19 November, on publication in the Federal
Register (the rule was released on 16 November). It closes the remote-access
loophole: licences are now required for advanced-chip exports to Indonesia,
Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and for data centres giving firms
headquartered in China or Macau (D:5) more than 12 million aggregate TPP of
compute access. The rule formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule;
existing data-centre arrangements have 90 days to obtain licences, and
comments are due 60 days after publication.
Sources: Gibson Dunn client alert (18 Nov); Baker McKenzie sanctions blog
(19 Nov); kurums.com (19 Nov).

## 12 — EU Parliament committee votes on the Space Act
The European Parliament's Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE)
was due to vote on 19 November on the European Commission's proposed Space
Act, the EU's first comprehensive space law, covering satellite safety,
debris and market rules.
Sources: Agence Europe, Europe Daily Bulletin No. 13984 (19 Nov — "the
Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE) will vote this Thursday");
Advanced Television (6 Nov — committee vote scheduled for 19 November).

## 13 — Colombia: national strike against the Economic Rescue Law
Trade unions led by the CUT and the teachers' federation Fecode called a
national strike for Thursday 19 November against the government's Economic
Rescue Law (bill 477), a fiscal adjustment bill combining spending cuts with
tax changes. Marches were scheduled in Bogotá (from Parque Nacional at 9:00
to Plaza de Bolívar), Medellín, Cali, Barranquilla and Bucaramanga; former
president Gustavo Petro said he would join the demonstrations. The US Embassy
in Bogotá issued a security alert on 17 November warning of traffic delays,
public-transport interruptions and temporary road blocks.
Sources: Colombia Unfiltered (18 Nov); The City Paper Bogotá (18 Nov);
US Embassy in Bogotá security alert (17 Nov); International Communist Press
(16 Nov).
