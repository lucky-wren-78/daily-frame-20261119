## The lead and the six cards

### 1 — APEC: Trump, Xi and Putin hold the first-ever trilateral and issue a "Declaration Towards Peace" (lead)
On the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir
Putin met for about 90 minutes at Kylin Villa — the first three-way meeting
of the three leaders — after separate Trump–Putin and Trump–Xi bilaterals.
They issued a four-point "Declaration Towards Peace": a nuclear war cannot
be won and must never be fought, with a dialogue on reducing nuclear risk;
more production and supply of refined petroleum products; reform of the UN
Security Council; and a warning that the International Criminal Court must
not abuse its jurisdiction. No timetable or figures were set, and Ukraine
was not explicitly mentioned.
Sources: USA Today (19 Nov); Reuters via Market Flux (19 Nov); Bernama via
PJM TV (19 Nov); Jagran Josh (19 Nov).

### 2 — ICJ: Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya — a first for the court
The International Court of Justice in The Hague ruled 9–6 that Myanmar
committed genocide against the Rohingya during its 2017 clearance operations
in northern Rakhine State — the first time in the court's 80-year history it
has held a state responsible for genocide. The court found about 10,000
Rohingya were violently killed and 392 villages burned in whole or part, and
ordered Myanmar to prosecute suspects, guarantee non-repetition, allow the
safe return of displaced Rohingya and pay reparations, with the amount to be
set in a later phase. The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019.
Sources: Le Monde (19 Nov); NPR via WEXT Radio (19 Nov); UN News / UNifeed
(19 Nov); Independent Investigative Mechanism for Myanmar (19 Nov).

### 3 — Anthropic's ANT opens at $111.50 and closes at $97.17, up 17% on day one
Anthropic's Class A shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market

### 5 — Houthi missile damages Riyadh's PIF Tower, the Saudi wealth fund's home
A Houthi missile struck the Public Investment Fund Tower — the 80-storey,
385-metre headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund and the
tallest tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District — blowing out
windows across seven to eight floors and bending parts of its metal facade.
Saudi civil defence confirmed material damage to several floors and nearby
buildings with no injuries; the Saudi-led coalition said it had dealt with a
missile threat towards Riyadh and that debris had fallen. It was the second
strike on the financial district in under two weeks, after Aramco's building
was hit on 8 November.
Sources: Reuters via The Daily Star / TBS News (19 Nov); Sowetan (19 Nov);
Arab News via The Library Estate (19 Nov).

### 6 — Bangladesh: Awami League lockdown day ends in arson, arrests and two ruling-party deaths
The banned Awami League's nationwide lockdown fell on 19 November, capping
a week of protests against the government's refusal of Sheikh Hasina's
request for a new passport so she can return in December. The shutdown had
mixed effect — Dhaka mostly kept moving — but crude-bomb blasts were reported
across six districts overnight, at least six buses and pickup vans were set
on fire, and the ancestral home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus in
Chattogram was torched. Police said two ruling BNP members were killed
overnight; 97 platoons of Border Guard Bangladesh were deployed across
seven districts, and 565 people were arrested in Dhaka in 24 hours,
including former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid.
Sources: Reuters via WIXX (19 Nov); The Voice (19 Nov); The Library Estate,
citing Reuters (19 Nov).

### 7 — California subpoenas Nvidia over its former chief's alleged affair with a suspected Chinese spy
California Attorney General Rob Bonta said on Thursday his office had served

## The briefs

### 8 — Kerch Bridge hit for a third time in a week; rail traffic halted, then partly restored
Ukrainian Neptune missiles struck the railway section of the 19-km Kerch
Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea overnight, halting train traffic;
Russia's transport ministry said nobody was hurt. The Moscow-appointed head
of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, said trains would resume from about 16:00 local
time, and one rail track reopened that afternoon. Road traffic had already
been impossible since Tuesday's strike collapsed a section of roadway.
Sources: Reuters (19 Nov); Associated Press via WPTF (19 Nov); Euronews via
Ekkow (19 Nov).

### 9 — EU Space Act: committee adopts its position 72–1, clearing the way to trilogue talks
The European Parliament's Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE)
adopted its report on the proposed EU Space Act by 72 votes to 1, with 2
abstentions, and voted by the same majority to open negotiations with the
Council. The text is about 22% shorter than the Commission's proposal — 97
articles instead of 119 — and drops the proposed Union-level legal
framework, according to rapporteur Elena Donazzan.
Sources: European Parliament news (19 Nov); EUobserver via EuropeSays
(19 Nov).

### 10 — COP31: presidency draft drops all fossil-fuel language; vulnerable nations protest
With the COP31 climate summit in Antalya entering its final hours, the
presidency released a second draft of the cover decision that removes all
direct references to fossil fuels and drops proposed roadmaps for moving away
from them. Civil society groups and delegates from climate-vulnerable
countries sharply criticised the draft; the presidency instead proposes
raising electricity's share of final energy use to 35% by 2035 and launches
the Antalya Compact for Energy Efficient Buildings.
Sources: IPS via Global Issues (19 Nov); Nation Press (19 Nov).

### 11 — Cyclone Akvan bears down on Odisha: 105,304 evacuated; landfall due Friday near Gopalpur
Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan sped up over the Bay of Bengal and, in the India
Meteorological Department's 11:30 am bulletin on 19 November, lay about
430 km south of Gopalpur, moving north at 17 km/h. It was expected to cross
the north Andhra Pradesh, Yanam and south Odisha coast between Narsapur and
Puri, close to Gopalpur, by noon on Friday 20 November, with winds of
90–100 km/h gusting to 110 km/h — down from the earlier very-severe forecast
of 120–130 km/h. Odisha had evacuated 105,304 people and shut schools in 11
districts; no deaths had been reported.
Sources: IMD bulletins via Wellthrise (19 Nov); The Proof News, citing IMD
Bulletin No. 31 (19 Nov).

### 13 — South Korea sits the CSAT: 551,864 candidates; 22 caught with phones
South Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test — the Suneung — was held on

### 16 — Marathon champion Jepchirchir banned three years for EPO; Sydney title stripped

### 17 — Sudan: court hands RSF leader Hemedti a second death sentence over a $396 million scandal

### 20 — US AI-chip rule takes effect; TSMC and Samsung pause some China shipments
The US Bureau of Industry and Security's interim final rule "Addressing
Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", issued 16 November, was published
in the Federal Register and took effect on 19 November. It requires licences
for advanced-chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and
Brazil, and for data centres giving D:5-headquartered companies remote
access to more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP of compute; it also formally
rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule. The Financial Times reported that TSMC and
Samsung have halted some chip deliveries to Chinese customers while they
seek licences; Reuters reported TSMC halting affected orders on 16nm
processes and below.
Sources: Financial Times via StockAnalysis and Stocktwits (19 Nov); Reuters
via MLQ.ai (19 Nov); Times of AI, citing the Federal Register (19 Nov).

### 30 — Kumamoto: magnitude 5.7 quake; Minamata orders all 22,133 residents to evacuate

## What was dropped during research

- A US nationwide FAA ground stop, widely re-reported on 19 November, was
  actually ordered on Friday 13 November after a telecommunications outage
  and lifted that evening; it is not a 19 November event and is not used.
- Italy's 5–4 World Cup qualifying win over Israel, with a stoppage-time
  Sandro Tonali winner, was played on 8 September 2025, not 19 November
  2026, and is not used.
- A "4 killed" figure for the Bangladesh violence, carried by one
  low-quality aggregator, could not be verified against any wire or national
  outlet and is not used anywhere in the image.
- The SpaceX Falcon 9 launch of the classified USSF-317 mission from
  Vandenberg was only ever reported as targeted for the evening of
  19 November (window from 20:28 PT); no confirmed liftoff was found before
  compilation, so the image says it was due, not that it flew.
- Several African items that appeared in 19 November digests with only one
  traceable source — a Kwara prison escape in Nigeria, a Senegal budget
  decree, a Kenya mosquito-net campaign, a South Africa "super El Niño"
  warning — were left out for failing the two-source standard.
