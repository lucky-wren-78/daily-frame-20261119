1 · SHENZHEN — APEC

Three powers, one table, a declaration of peace

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin sat down together for the first time at Kylin Villa, Shenzhen — about 90 minutes of talks on the sidelines of APEC.

They left with a four-point “Declaration Towards Peace”: a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought; more refined petroleum output; UN Security Council reform; and a warning that the ICC must not abuse its jurisdiction. No dates, no figures — and no mention of Ukraine.

2THE HAGUE · LAW

A court calls it genocide — the first time ever for a state

The International Court of Justice ruled 9–6 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017 — the first time in the court’s 80-year history it has held a state responsible. About 10,000 Rohingya were killed and 392 villages burned; Myanmar must prosecute suspects and pay reparations.

3NEW YORK · MARKETS

Anthropic’s ANT opens at $111.50, closes at $97.17 — up 17%

The biggest IPO ever hit the Nasdaq: 980 million shares at $83, about $81.3 billion raised. The first trade printed 34% above the IPO price, the shares peaked at $115.10 and closed at $97.17; 846 million shares traded — the day’s busiest name. The Nasdaq fell 2.25%.

4WORLDWIDE · GAMES

GTA VI launches — about 19 million copies, $1.84 billion

Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale at midnight in every region, beginning in New Zealand, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. No official figures, but Sensor Tower put launch-day sales at 19.05 million copies and about $1.84 billion — more than twice GTA V’s first day in 2013.

5RIYADH · CONFLICT

Houthi missile hits the tower of Saudi Arabia’s wealth fund

A missile struck the PIF Tower — the 80-storey, 385-metre headquarters of the Saudi sovereign wealth fund — blowing out windows across seven to eight floors. Civil defence reported material damage and no injuries; the coalition said it dealt with a missile threat.

6DHAKA · UNREST

Bangladesh lockdown day: arson, 565 arrests, two killed

The banned Awami League’s nationwide lockdown had mixed effect — Dhaka mostly kept moving — but crude bombs went off in six districts, buses were torched and the ancestral home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus was set alight. Two ruling-party members were killed; 97 Border Guard platoons deployed, 565 arrested in Dhaka.

7CALIFORNIA · CHIPS

California subpoenas Nvidia over its ex-chief’s alleged affair

Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia over reports that former CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Huang resigned last week after revelations of affairs, two with Nvidia employees; the board has ruled him out, and the SEC is said to be probing the firm.

THE REST OF THE DAY — IN BRIEF

8Kerch Bridge hit again. Neptune missiles hit the railway section overnight, halting trains; one track reopened by afternoon.

9EU Space Act advances. Parliament’s industry committee adopted its position 72–1 and voted to open trilogue talks; the text is ~22% shorter than the draft.

10COP31 draft drops fossil fuels. The Antalya presidency’s second cover text removes all fossil-fuel references and phase-out roadmaps.

11Cyclone Akvan closes in. Odisha evacuated 105,304 people; landfall is due near Gopalpur by noon Friday at 90–100 km/h.

12India–Japan 2+2. Tokyo talks fast-track the UNICORN naval mast; Japanese troops will train in India in 2027.

13Korea’s big exam day. 551,864 candidates sat the CSAT; 22 were caught with phones after a disaster alert rang unsubmitted handsets.

14NZ fight back in Wellington. From 70/5, Henry Nicholls (113*) and Glenn Phillips (94*) put on an unbroken 173; New Zealand closed day one on 243/5.

15Proteas top the table. Harmer’s 6/51 bowled Bangladesh out for 168; South Africa won by nine wickets, topping the Test championship.

16Marathon champion banned. Peres Jepchirchir got a three-year EPO ban; her Sydney title is stripped, Cheptai elevated. She will miss LA 2028.

17Hemedti sentenced again. A Port Sudan court handed the RSF leader a second death sentence in absentia over a $396m looting case.

18Colombia strikes. Unions marched nationwide against the Economic Rescue Law, which would lift VAT on some goods from 5% to 19%.

19Para table tennis gold. England’s Joshua Morizono won the men’s class 9 world title in Pattaya, beating Ma Lin 3–2 — 11–9 in the fifth.

20US chip rule bites. The remote-access AI-chip rule took effect; TSMC and Samsung paused some China shipments pending US licences.

21Nasdaq slides. The Composite fell 2.25% (550.69 points) to 23,879.20 on the day the biggest IPO ever debuted.

22$56.7bn of gold, undeclared. SWISSAID estimates the UAE imported $56.7 billion of African gold that never appeared in the exporters’ statistics.

23Sudan’s drone toll. The UN rights office confirmed 2,230+ civilian deaths in Sudan this year, 1,600+ in drone strikes.

24Belgium 4–3 Wales. Wales fought back from 3–0 down in Brussels to level, but De Bruyne’s late winner ended their unbeaten run.

25Qualifiers round-up. Germany beat Slovakia 6–0 to top Group A; Northern Ireland beat Luxembourg 1–0; Scotland lost 3–2 in Greece.

26Australia’s jobless jump. Unemployment rose to 4.9%, a five-year high, with ~50,000 jobs lost in October; the ASX 200 fell 1.78%.

27Sandpapergate, revisited. Cameron Bancroft said the whole Australian team knew of the 2018 ball-tampering plot; Cummins declined to answer.

28Epstein files, one year on. A year after the Transparency Act passed 427–1, survivors still await millions of files; a second act may get a vote by end-November.

29Canberra passes defence bill. Australia’s Senate passed the 231-page defence bill, letting the minister draw moving security zones around bases.

30Kumamoto shakes. A magnitude 5.7 quake struck at 02:22 JST, no tsunami threat; Minamata ordered all 22,133 residents to evacuate.
